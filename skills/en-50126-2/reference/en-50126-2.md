# EN 50126-2:2017 — Systems Approach to Safety — Full Reference

BS EN 50126-2:2017 (CENELEC Oct 2017), "Railway applications — RAMS — Part 2: Systems Approach to Safety". Supersedes and makes obsolete CLC/TR 50126-2:2007. Normative reference: EN 50126-1:2017 (terms/definitions of Part 1 apply). Selected methods' mandatory requirements become mandatory when the method is selected. BSI English text; German/French editions exist. Frequency/severity categories and the tolerability matrix are calibrated in **EN 50126-1:2017 Annex C** (not restated here). Annex ZZ: TSI essential-requirements support.

## 1. Framework & structure
- EN 50126 = railway-sector application of IEC 61508. Part 2 supports Part 1's Safety Management Process with **methods**: safety specification/demonstration, SPI discipline, risk assessment details, safety requirements derivation, safety integrity, SIL derivation for safety-related electronic functions.
- Scope: safety-related generic RAMS life-cycle aspects; three application fields (CCS / Rolling Stock / Fixed Installations); new & modified systems; does not define application-specific targets/certification/approval processes.

## 2. Hourglass model & system approach
- **Fig. 1 Hourglass (5.1)**: A **Risk assessment** at railway-system level (Phases 1–3): system definition (black box; boundary hazards only; "unintentional train motion" example) → risk analysis (hazard identification; consequence analysis; RAP selection) → risk evaluation. B **Outcome** (Phase 4; the waist = **System Requirement Specification**): listed hazards + associated safety measures; safety requirements (from selected CoPs; ERE objectives; reference-system specs). C **Hazard control** at supplied-system level (Phases 5–9): refined hazard identification, **causal analysis**, **Common Cause Analysis**, verification/compliance demonstration. D **Revision loop**: new hazards in hazard control → risk assessment revision (incl. application conditions).
- **Technical system** excludes human operators (their actions restricted via application conditions). Bow-tie (Fig. 2): several causes → hazard → accidents; hazard of system-under-consideration = a cause of railway-system hazard; **external occurrence barriers** recorded in the risk model and become **SRAC** (missed-condition risk acceptance = safety accepted with specific conditions).
- Risk model (Fig. 7, 8.2.2): many-to-many causes–hazards–accidents–consequences, with operational/environmental context and triggering events. Top-down (accident targets → THR) or bottom-up (THR → accident targets) both allowed (8.4.1.3).

## 3. Safety management & organisation
- Responsibility split (5.6): risk assessment mainly **railway duty holder** (may be contracted to competent manufacturers; should accept outcomes); **supplier responsible for hazard control**; several suppliers ⇒ duty holder organises overall hazard control.
- Development types / safety cases (Cl. 6): **Generic Product (GP)** application-independent — GPSC; **Generic Application (GA)** application class — GASC; **Specific Application (SA)** incl. physical implementation — **SASC** (may split: application design / physical implementation). Evidence: system definition & context; requirements incl. safety requirements (+risk analysis where applicable); safety case; **ISA report** when required. GP validation at end of Phase 6 (first item) as well as Phase 9?? — **at Phase 6 end for GP/GA** per 6.2 wording (design and implementation incl. manufacture of first item). Sub-system acceptance precedes main system acceptance (dependencies fixed Phase 2). SA acceptance may be by technical equivalence to an accepted application (still needs SASC). Modifications post-acceptance (6.4) processed as new design; safety case updated; continued safety per Safety Plan safety monitoring.
- **Independence of roles (Cl. 7, Figs. 5–6)**: organisation shall implement EN ISO 9001-equivalent process plus: an **ISA** shall be appointed with authority where required — **independent from the Project Manager and a different entity from all other project roles**; Phases 1–4: verification of each phase's results by entity not involved; system/safety requirements **validated** by competent entity not involved in their development; Validator independent from PM; (Verifier and Validator may come from same entity, mutual PM independence preserved); Phases 5+: Case (A) full independence structure; Case (B) reduced structure allowed only where risk analysis showed **no credible direct potential of at least one fatality**, or, where applicable, **apportioned SIL ≤ 2**. Same-role conflict: outputs peer-reviewed.
- **Competence (7.4, Annex G)**: role specs for Designer, Verifier, Validator, ISA, Project Manager (responsibilities + key competencies); QMS defines required experience/capability/qualification; documented evidence; continuous maintenance (logbook).

## 4. Risk evaluation (8.4)
- Quantitative ERE (8.4.1): risk = frequency × severity of accident scenarios (FTA for hazard frequencies; ETA for hazard→accident probability).
- **Variability (8.4.2)**: worst possible scenario / **reasonable estimates (recommended)** / reasonable worst case.
- **Qualitative (8.4.3)**: category-judgement; calibrated via EN 50126-1:2017 Annex C.
- Risk tolerance table continuity: categories and matrix in Part 1 Annex C.

## 5. THR / TFFR / SIL machinery (Cl. 10)
- **THR = Tolerable Hazard Rate** (per hazard, expressed per hour; transformations if targets given per function). THR relates to **specific elementary functions without considering the number of instances** in the railway system (so fleet-size growth doesn't invalidate acceptance). Duty holder responsibilities: specify THR, define quantitative targets at railway-system level, define system+context, pass THR. Supplier: confirm assumptions, demonstrate hazard rates meet THR, document assumptions as **application conditions**.
- **TFFR = Tolerable Functional unsafe Failure Rate** (per function, per hour). THR → TFFR via causal analysis (fault trees etc.) down to the *level of the last independent functions* (counteract regardless of the others; demonstrated freedom from random and systematic CCFs; AND-gate needs detailed demonstration incl. detection/negation contributions).
- If freedom from **both** random and systematic CCFs → apportion further, allocate **SIL** at the lowest totally independent level. If only random-CCF-free → apportion failure rates but **SIL stays with the parent function** (qualitative measures unchanged) (10.2.9). Multiple hazards per function → separate derivation; **most restrictive applies** (10.2.8).
- **Table 2 (TFFR → SIL, exact)**:
  | TFFR [h⁻¹] | SIL |
  |---|---|
  | 10⁻⁹ ≤ TFFR < 10⁻⁸ | 4 |
  | 10⁻⁸ ≤ TFFR < 10⁻⁷ | 3 |
  | 10⁻⁷ ≤ TFFR < 10⁻⁶ | 2 |
  | 10⁻⁶ ≤ TFFR < 10⁻⁵ | 1 |
  - TFFR < 10⁻⁹: split into independent functions and apportion, OR implement **at least SIL 4 measures** + other technical/operational measures to reach the target.
  - TFFR ≥ 10⁻⁵: attribute **Basic Integrity**.
  - Quantitative attribution covers **random** integrity only; systematic failures covered by the SIL qualitative measures (QM + SM + technical safety conditions; "defined in sector-specific standards"). SIL achievement = quantitative target + qualitative measures (Fig. 11).
- **SIL only for electronic functions** (10.2.1); non-electronic functions use CoPs/technology standards; a SIL given to electromechanical equipment is treated as not-applicable and stated as such. Demand-mode: transform any on-demand probabilities to continuous-rate models; TFFR in h⁻¹.
- **TFFR alone insufficient (10.2.4)** — also address: fault detection facilities, safety design principles for safe reaction, **SDT (safe down time)** if used in the model, safe-state exit (restoration or safe state), containment/recovery, procedural/maintenance actions with periodicity, auxiliary diagnostics/PM.
- **SIL misuse prevention (10.2.12, Annex C Q&A)**: SIL only after risk analysis at the right phases; assigned to the **last independent function**, never to systems ("SIL 4 computer" wrong); never for non-functional safety (slips/trips/falls); restricted-subsystem sharing: highest SIL or mutual non-intrusiveness; fulfilling quantitative+qualitative requirements does not guarantee the safety requirements are correct.

## 6. Apportionment formulas (Annex D.3)
- OR-gate logic: Σ apportioned THRs ≤ total THR.
- **Formula 2 (two independent repairable functions X,Y with rates λ, repair rates µ):** THR_A ≈ THR_X·THR_Y·(µ_X+µ_Y)/(µ_X·µ_Y).
- **Formula 1 (detection/negation-dominated):** THR_A ≈ THR_X·THR_Y·(SDR_X+SDR_Y)/(SDR_X·SDR_Y), SDR ≈ 1/SDT.
- **Formula 4 (independent barrier G with detection+negation protecting function F, SDR_G=SDR_P):** THR_A ≈ THR_F·THR_G·SDR/(THR_F+THR_G).
- AND-gate (probabilities): P_B·P_C = (1−e^(−λ_B·SDT_B))(1−e^(−λ_C·SDT_C)) ≈ λ_B·λ_C·SDT_B·SDT_C — valid only if λ·SDT ≪ 1; conservative SDT assumptions required.
- **Worked example (Fig. D.6):** THR_A = 10⁻⁹/h ⇒ THR_B ≈ 10⁻⁷/h with SDT_B = 15 y (~108 000 h) and THR_C ≈ 10⁻⁷/h with SDT_C = 12 h (λ_B·λ_C = 9.26×10⁻¹⁵ h⁻²).
- ** Semi-quantitative barrier efficiency (D.2):** E = E_F × E_K × E_U × E_M with graded tables:
  - E_F failure history: I never failed in earlier systems' lifetimes; II few failures (<1 per system); III ≥1 per lifetime; IV ≥1/year across systems; V ≥1/year per system.
  - E_K knowledge: I widely used in the company; II used elsewhere; III not used but replaces a used component; IV unknown.
  - E_U use: I same applications; II similar only; III different constraints.
  - E_M maintenance: I daily-tested, no use before repair; II online test, repair ≤1 week; III ≤1 month; IV ≤6 months; V ≤1 year; VI ≤ mid-life; VII untested/no repair whole life.
  - (E_F=1 advised against — reserved for criteria-based preventive maintenance, e.g. bogies.)
- **Common quantification mistakes (Annex E):** mixing rates with probabilities in AND gates (must be probabilities: rate × SDT); ignoring test intervals in OR; e.g. "periodic-test 1e-6 10 0" ⇒ λ=10⁻⁶/h with test interval 10 h; out-of-range formulas when λ_eq·T_eq not ≪ 1.

## 7. Acceptance criteria methods (Annex A)
- **ALARP (A.2)**: tolerance zone + required cost-benefit balance; CBA of measure cost vs risk reduction (refs: RSSB "Taking Safe Decisions" 2008); collective-risk place; a risk may be tolerable but not ALARP.
- **GAME (A.3, France STRMTG law)**: new/modified system **globally at least as safe** as an accepted reference; collective + individual risks; qualitative or quantitative demonstration; application conditions must match for equivalence.
- **MEM (A.4, Kuhlmann 1981)**: natural minimum death rate **R_m = 2×10⁻⁴ fatalities/(person·year)** (ages 5–15); total technical risk ≤ R_m; ≤20 parallel systems each ≤ 5% ⇒ **R ≤ 10⁻⁵ fatality/(person·year)** per system; **DRA** curve relaxes tolerability with accident size (tolerated individual risk falls from 10⁻⁵ at 1 fatality through 10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁷ … toward ~10⁻⁹–10⁻¹⁰ near 100–200 fatalities); **equivalent weighting 1 fatality = 10 major = 100 minor** injuries. Table A.1 compares ALARP/GAME/MEM across approach/reference/assumptions/criteria/application/strengths/weaknesses. RAP choice requires stakeholder agreement.

## 8. Safety requirements (Cl. 9)
- **"Safety-related"** (9.1): function/component/procedure whose ≥1 property is used in the safety argument, or has ≥1 safety requirement assigned.
- Specification content (9.2): safety-related functions; safety assumptions (mitigation barrier effectiveness: PFD, per-hour, etc.); **THR/TFFR** quantitative requirements, considering: definition of safe states; max time to enter safe state; failure detection facilities; upper-level hazard-analysis requirements; interface adaptation; organisational/operational/maintenance rules; environmental conditions; legal safety requirements. Agreed between stakeholders; assessed holistically (residual risk as a whole).
- **Categorisation (9.3, Fig. 9)**: (a) **functional safety requirements**: expected behaviour of safety-related functions + behaviour on non-hazardous failures; split into **safety integrity** requirements (random part: diversity, redundancy, environmental protection; systematic part: QM, SM, organisational measures — "no commonly accepted basis for quantifying systematic failures", so methods preclude their quantification) and **required behaviour** (enforcement/retention of safe state); (b) **technical safety requirements**: fire, sharp edges, electric voltage, combustible materials, structural integrity, harmful substances, mechanical strength, failures under moisture/heat/fire, insulation coordination, earthing, EMC, conformity with external standards; (c) **contextual safety requirements**: operational (personnel action; normal/abnormal operation procedures; operational restrictions — speed, number of trains, signalling instructions; human-interface rules incl. training) and maintenance (intervals, rules, procedures, pre-service test/check set, spare-part storage limits, tool restrictions).

## 9. Hazard control (Cl. 11)
- **Causal analysis** (11.2): identify causal factors (environment, function, interface, HW, SW, human) + logic relations, incl. common-cause modelling. **Refined hazard identification** (11.3): new technology/transfer/mistakes/special operation modes/design errors; **all outcomes recorded in the hazard log and referred to in the safety case**.
- **Common Cause Analysis** (11.4): model via OR gate; on CCF identification either design change or demonstrate THR still acceptable including CCF; check external and internal interfaces; measures ensuring physical and logical independence (non-intentional influences).
- Techniques (11.5): FTA (IEC 61025:2010), **FMECA (IEC 60812:2006)**, Markov (IEC 61165:2006), ETA (IEC 62502:2010), HAZOP (IEC 61882:2001), **RBD (IEC 61078:2006)**, human-factor analysis.

## 11. Basic Integrity regime (10.2.11)
Early phases: risk-analysis evaluation traced in hazard log; independence requirements apply; Phase 5: fault management (diagnostics, maintenance, operator training, procedures); Phase 8: assumptions as SRAC, non-intrusiveness on SIL functions shown, impacts analyzed; Phase 9: safety case addresses it; Phase 11: follow-up through lifecycle (maintenance inspections verifying random-failure target). Evidence of quality: effective EN ISO 9001 / ISO/IEC 90003 compliance; operator training + maintenance manuals; Verifier/Validator different from Designer but **team organisational independence not required**; self-assessment acceptable; no additional safety case required for BI itself.

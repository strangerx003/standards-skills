# CLC/TR 50126-3:2006 (PD CLC/TR 50126-3:2006) — Rolling Stock RAM Guide — Full Reference

PD CLC/TR 50126-3:2006 (BSI edition, published 30 June 2006; CENELEC TR approved 2004-08-28, published May 2006), "Railway applications — The specification and demonstration of Reliability, Availability, Maintainability and Safety (RAMS) — Part 3: Guide to the application of EN 50126-1 for rolling stock RAMS". Prepared by Working Group B11 of SC 9XB (Electromechanical material on board of rolling stock) of CENELEC TC 9X. **Informative Technical Report** — forms an informative part of EN 50126. All references to EN 50126-1 are to the **1998 edition** (Scope, Clause 1). ICS 29.280; 45.020.

## 1. Scope, status & relation to EN 50126-1:1998 (Cl. 1, 1.1, 4)

- **Covers only RAM** (not Safety) of EN 50126-1 as applied to rolling stock, from **invitation to tender to demonstration in operation**. EN 50126-1 itself is "essentially aimed as a top level railway system document" (Introduction) — this guide clarifies areas where it "could be misinterpreted".
- **Applicability (4.1):** rolling stock (train, coach, locomotive, etc.) and all its subsystems, assemblies and parts according to boundary limits; use by Railway Authorities and railway support industry for:
  - **new rolling stock**;
  - **modification/refurbishment of existing rolling stock**.
  The two bullet items are generically termed "**system**" = complete sequence "system, sub-system, component" of a rolling stock or the whole rolling stock breakdown structure.
- **Purpose (Cl. 1)** — enable the customer/operator to: specify RAM requirements addressing the type of operation in terms of end-customer needs (service availability + economics); evaluate tenders on a common basis; gain assurance during design/development via step-by-step specific RAM documents; validate that delivered stock satisfies requirements. Enable the main supplier to: understand customer RAM requirements; show in tender via preliminary RAM analysis that the product is likely to satisfy RAM requirements; provide substantive information during design/development via detailed RAM analysis; demonstrate that the delivered product satisfies the RAM requirements.
- **Limitation of scope (1.1).** Restricted to: (a) Safety — only a reference list of common rolling stock hazards (Cl. 7); (b) LCC — only the key RAM parameters for an LCC model (Cl. 8). **Excludes** RAM values (gives only a guideline process for choosing values, 5.4) and specific mandatory RAM documents (provides only example templates, 5.5.4).
- **Application of EN 50126-1 (4.2.1, per 5.3.2/5.3.4 of EN 50126-1):** Railway Authority defines the assessment; it shall (1) specify applicable phases and for each justify compliance, specify mandatory activities/requirements (scope of each requirement; methods/tools/techniques + scope & depth; verification/validation activities; input/output documentation); (2) justify any deviation; (3) justify adequacy of tasks chosen. Customer and Main Supplier both provide documented evidence; supplier-introduced limitations should be discussed/agreed; these documents are part of the RAM Programme.
- **Mandatory requirements (4.2.2, synthesis of 5.3.5 of EN 50126-1):** define/agree responsibilities for all RAMS tasks per phase; ensure competence of personnel; establish/implement a **RAM Programme and a Safety Plan** (identify & manage conflicts between RAM and Safety tasks; details of RAMS analyses incl. depth); implement EN 50126-1 requirements within business processes supported by a QMS compliant with **EN ISO 9000 series**; establish an **effective configuration management system** covering all system documentation and deliverables.
- **Approach (Cl. 3):** for each life-cycle phase ask **WHAT** (activities/tasks + documents), **WHO** (responsibility), **HOW** (methods/tools).
- **Entities (Introduction, Table 1 — possible sharing of responsibility):** Main supplier of rolling stock — supplier; Sub-supplier — supplier; Operator — customer; Owner — customer; **Maintainer — both supplier and customer responsibility** (X in both columns).

## 2. Life-cycle & roles (Cl. 6, Fig. 7–9)

- **Fig. 7** maps the RAM Programme onto the EN 50126-1:1998 14 phases: 1 Concept; 2 System definition and application conditions; 3 Risk Analysis; 4 System Requirements; 5 Apportionment of system requirement; 6 Design and implementation; 7 Manufacture; 8 Installation; 9 System validation; 10 System acceptance; 11 Operation and maintenance; 12 Performance monitoring; 13 Modification and retrofit; 14 Decommissioning and disposal.
- **Subclause 5** (Specifying RAM requirements) details **phases 1, 2, 4, 5** (per 6.1, 6.2, 6.4, 6.5 of EN 50126-1); **6.1.2** details **phase 6** (6.6); **6.1.3** details **phases 9, 10, 11** (6.9, 6.10, 6.11). Clause 6 "RAM Assurance during Life Cycle" is the bridge: deliverables of each phase form inputs to subsequent phases and results of each phase must be confirmed in successive phases.
- **Fig. 8** shows possible Customer / Main-Contractor / Sub-Contractor relationships: Customer performs call for tender, feasibility & risk analysis, mission profile & operating rules, apportionment of RS performances, negotiation, decision; Main Supplier performs concept, generic rolling-stock definition & application conditions, risk analysis, RS performances, apportionment, RAM Programme Plan, RAM preliminary/detailed analyses, consolidation, performance growth monitoring, RAM Demonstration, RAM Synthesis Reports; Equipment Supplier performs concept, equipment definition & application conditions, risk analysis, system performances, apportionment for equipment, detailed analyses, RAM synthesis reports, implementation of action plans. Gates shown: Preliminary Design Review → Final Design Review → Final acceptance.
- **RAM Programme (5.5.1):** a set of activities over the whole Life Cycle to ensure RAM requirements are fulfilled at each development phase; established and maintained by **both** Main Supplier and Customer; each entity plans activities within its responsibility; managed via **periodic reviews** consistent with 4.2/4.2.2. **Main Supplier is responsible for the RAM Programme content and Plan** and submits them for Customer acceptance per the System Quality Plan.
- **Configuration Management System (5.5.2):** established and maintained by the Main Supplier; identifies/documents modifications to functional/physical characteristics; records & reports processing, implementation status and verification of compliance with RAM requirements. Periodic review reports specify at minimum: documents delivered with revisions; status (carried out / in progress / to be carried out) with objective evidence; notification of problems affecting RAM requirements; proposals for action. Control applies to all project phases — from **Phase 10 to Phases 11+ the Customer should also establish a CMS** (ref. EN 50126-1 Fig. 9 Note 1).

## 3. Preliminary RAM analysis (5.1; complements 6.1.3.4, 6.2.3.1, 6.2.3.2, 6.4.3.1 and A.2 of EN 50126-1)

- **Purpose (5.1.1):** identify application environment and operating conditions to establish the fundamental concepts for the overall RAM requirements. Activities: **similar system review** (list of similar existing rolling stock for extracting RAM-related data) and **preliminary system analysis** (review documentation to define system structure, mission profile and failure conditions at preliminary level).
- **Deliverables (5.1.2)** feed the RAM Requirements Specification in three blocks: RAM requirements; **demonstration and acceptance criteria**; RAM programme requirements. Two deliverables: **System identification** and **Failure conditions**.
- **System identification (5.1.3)** — reference conditions for both defining RAM requirements and demonstrating compliance in all life-cycle phases. Five groups:
  1. **Mission Profile:** reference route; commercial speed (mission duration/length); mean length of a run; mean distance between train stops; operating time or distance per year; revenue operating time/distance per year; stand-by time per day; idle time per day; planned total time of use (life expectancy in years).
  2. **Route Profile:** number of tunnels and viaducts on the reference route; cumulative distance in tunnels and on surface incl. viaducts; gradients and curves with lengths.
  3. **Operating Conditions:** equivalent speed (cumulated distance / powered time over the period); powered equipment time per calendar period (per equipment or per category); traction-activation time; time/percentage of electric braking; compressor operating time and number of starts; catenary-power presence time per voltage; trainset-awake time (operation or standby); HVAC operating time (heating / air-conditioning modes); average and maximum speed; use in multiple units; total coupled-operation time and coupling/decoupling frequency; internal temperature range; mechanical shock & vibration; electrical power supplies; EMC (train-to-train, system-to-system); signalling interfaces (on-board and way-side); ergonomics.
  4. **Environmental Conditions:** outside air temperature (OAT); max altitude; solar radiation; humidity; wind and pressure pulses; water/precipitation; pollutants and contaminants. (Cf. bibliography: EN 60721-1.)
  5. **Maintenance Conditions:** indicative maintenance plan (minimum preventive-maintenance interval, max contemporary personnel per interval task, max standstill time per interval task, etc.); number/location/description of maintenance sites; standard equipment, tools and resources at the sites.
- **Breakdown structure & boundary limits (5.1.4):** baseline of the identification process; sets system borders by listing all items and discrete breakdown levels. Two categories:
  - **Functional breakdown structure** — for preliminary **criticality** analyses; the last functional level develops consequences of functional failure modes, the next physical level develops the critical-item list.
  - **Physical breakdown structure** (aka **logistic** breakdown structure) — for **maintainability** analyses; last level = **LRU (Line Replaceable Unit)**.
- **Rules for the physical breakdown (5.1.4.1):** top-down decomposition from rolling stock as system under consideration; each level's system becomes the next system under consideration; recommendations: limit levels to a **reasonable number (three or four suggested)**; last item on a branch must be a **LRU**; same terms/definitions for identical items; terms identical in all design documents; avoid continuous modification after first issue; avoid vague definitions.
- **Minimum data (5.1.4.2, Tables 2 and 3):** header = Rolling stock ID, Doc. N., Version number, Date, Page n/N, Drawn up by, Filename. Item data = **Code** (level code), **Description**, **Qty** (per higher-level item), **Part No.** (recommended for every item). Examples in Annex A.

## 4. Failure conditions & categories (5.2; complements 4.5.2.2 and 6.4.3.2 of EN 50126-1)

General failure conditions defined: **Immobilising**, **Service**, **Minor** failures. **Table 4 — RAM Failure Categories:**

| Category | EN 50126-1 label | Definition (Table 4) |
|---|---|---|
| **Significant (Immobilising Failure)**, symbol **FailI** | Significant | A failure that (a) prevents train movement or causes a delay to service **greater than a specified time** and/or (b) generates a **cost greater than a specified level** |
| **Major (Service Failure)**, symbol **FailS** | Major | A failure that (a) **must be rectified** for the rolling stock to achieve its specified performance and (b) does **not** cause a delay or cost greater than the minimum threshold specified for a significant failure |
| **Minor**, symbol **FailM** | Minor | A failure that (a) does not prevent the rolling stock achieving its specified performance and (b) meets no criteria for Significant or Major failures |

Rolling-stock-specific conditions (5.2): **Significant** — any failure leading to at least one of: delay greater than a specified time; **train stopped on the track**; train not allowed to enter service; withdrawal of the train from service; cost greater than a specified threshold. **Major** — delay less than a specified time; loss of specific functions or of performance below the specified minimum acceptable level; cost less than a specified threshold. **Minor** — any failure that is neither significant nor major and leads to a **maintenance task even with no impact on service**.

**What the Customer must state:** the delay minutes for Significant and Major failures; threshold costs; the **method for counting delay** (at end of run only / cumulated over all stops / the maximum between two stops, etc.); specific service conditions deemed to cause train stop or withdrawal; functions and performance thresholds classed as Major failures (examples given: passenger air-conditioning failure, driver's cab air-conditioning failure, door system failure **per side**, failure of a specified number of toilet systems, coach lighting failure).

**Tables 5–7 — specification templates per category.** Table 5 (Significant): conditions "delay greater than" (Time — specify mode of counting delay), "train stopped on the track" (Rolling Stock unable to proceed under its own power), "withdrawal from service / not allowed to enter" (specify special service conditions), "cost greater than" (Cost — specify reference cost). Table 6 (Major): "delay less than" (Time), "failing specified performance" (specify services/functions and performance threshold levels), "cost less than" (Cost). Table 7 (Minor): any failure on the rolling stock (Time dimension). One or more conditions per category may be applied.

**Counting for demonstration:** failures are counted per category against the reliability targets (5.3.2) and the chargeable/non-chargeable rules (6.1.3.2). Criticality of failure modes is recorded in the FMECA (5.5.4.6) as a **Criticality** code (safety and/or service effects) per a stated reference table, plus the **Failure Category** (FailI/S/M) per failing LRU or function.

## 5. Reliability targets (5.3.2, Table 8)

- Targets apply to the **total rolling stock** and to all subsystems/assemblies/parts per boundary limits, for each Failure Category: **Maximum accepted Failure Rate (FR)** in failure/million km or failure/million hours, and **minimum accepted MTBF / MTTF / MDBF** in hours or kilometres. Hours/kilometres = **hours/kilometres of service**.
- **MTBF for repairable units; MTTF for non-repairable units.** If real service operating time cannot be measured, the Customer can instead specify **maximum accepted failure rate + minimum accepted MDBF** (mean distance between failures). **Converting MTBF ↔ MDBF requires a predefined average speed** derived from Mission Profile requirements (5.1.3).
- **Important systems/subsystems:** the Customer may additionally specify targets per system/subsystem, with subsystem-specific failure definitions: any failure on a defined system/subsystem leading to failure to meet a **specified performance**, and/or any failure leading to a **maintenance task** even with no service impact; the Customer should state the failure conditions in which the subsystem fails its specified performances.
- **Table 8 structure:** rows = Rolling Stock; System/Subsystem 1 … System/Subsystem n; columns per row = Max Accepted Failure Rate (Number/Million km or /Million hours, symbol **FR** with subscripts **I/S/M**) and MTBF/MTTF/MDBF (Hours or Kilometres, symbols **MTBF_I/S/M**, **MTTF_I/S/M**, **MDBF_I/S/M**). Indexes **I, S, M** = Immobilising, Service, Minor categories. Separate tables may be established per failure category; subsystem targets must reference the Breakdown Structure (5.1.4).
- **Apportionment:** this guide does **not** prescribe apportionment formulas. It appears only as: a Phase-5 life-cycle activity ("apportionment of system requirements", Fig. 7), a tier-transfer activity (apportionment of RS performances from Customer to Main Supplier, then apportionment for equipment to sub-suppliers, Fig. 8), a RAM Programme Plan content item ("**Reliability Modelling, prediction and apportionment**", 5.5.3), the RAM specification step where the Main Supplier passes contractual requirements down to sub-contractors/partners/equipment suppliers (6.1.2, Table 19), and the integration step of detailed analyses: collect sub-contractor analyses, ensure consistency, integrate their equipment-level predictions into the model to obtain rolling-stock-level predictions, and integrate **interface mismatches** (6.1.2). Supported methods named: **Reliability Block Diagrams**, **Fault Tree Analysis** (tender feasibility, 6.1.1), functional **FMECA** (phases 1–5 vs detailed analyses), and the aggregation arithmetic of the prediction sheets (sum of LRU failure rates × quantities, Table 15).
- **Target values:** the guide gives **no numeric RAM values** (explicit exclusion, 1.1). Choosing figures (5.4): analyse system identification & breakdown structure; consider functional requirements and related subsystems; derive requirements by analysing functional requirements for their failure conditions; consider RAM requirements and **past achieved RAM performances** of similar rolling stock; consider technical evolution, impact of new functionalities, a **realistic improvement** of RAM requirements, and other documented needs. The Customer should document the process and the consideration given to each figure.

## 6. Maintainability targets (5.3.3, Tables 9–12)

Five target types: generic qualitative requirements; **preventive maintenance** requirements (qualitative/quantitative: frequency, max personnel per frequency, max hours per frequency); **corrective maintenance** requirements (MTTR, Maximum TTR); **logistic support** requirements (supply/administrative delay, spare-parts availability); **maintenance cost** requirements.

**Table 9 — Qualitative Requirements for Maintainability** (each is an essay-style requirement): **Accessibility** (layout for easy inspection/repair/revision/replacement with tool dimensions, working area, safety standards, localised illumination; fastening points and interfacing points — e.g. ventilation channels, fans, filters — accessible without special tools); **Dismounting** (remove any item without operating on other items not involved; account for hatch/casing removal and ease of opening/handling); **Modularity** (reduce intervention time, personnel specialisation and spares stock; enables two-level maintenance: **primary = rapid replacement on the unit, secondary = repair off the train** in the workshop); **Handling** (no item should exceed the weight supportable by a single person / manually handled by one person; maximum weight may be **doubled** where two persons can work together; shape must not be hazardous; provisions for hooking/hoisting with forklift — eyebolts, hooks, feet — designed/supplied under **Main Supplier responsibility** if not commercially available; connections accessible, disconnectable, reconnectable, corrosion-resistant); **Ease of cleaning** (incl. textile coverings, carpeting designed for easy off-train replacement, avoiding sharp corners/hollows where dirt accumulates — verified on the mock-up; Main Supplier indicates compatible cleaners); **Standardisation** (interchange with parts already in use by the Customer and on other rolling stock; commercial or unified solutions for elementary objects; Customer supplies a list of already-used parts and may restrict sole use; close match between drawings and realisation of pneumatic/electric circuits across the fleet); **Interchangeability** (remove one item and install another without affecting equipment characteristics — **compatible in form, fit and function**); **Testability** (clear, rapid condition identification; preventive diagnostics before failure and corrective diagnostics to identify the malfunctioning item).

**Table 10 — Preventive/Corrective Maintenance Requirements** (definition / dimension / proposed symbol):

| Definition | Dimension | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum interval for scheduled maintenance | hours, kilometres | MinInt |
| Maximum standstill time to complete maintenance actions | hours | MaxST |
| Maximum allowed number of personnel employed for maintenance | dimensionless | MaxANP |
| Maximum man-hours for a specific maintenance action | hours | MaxMMH |
| Maximum allowed time for dismounting/mounting | hours | MaxAT |
| Maximum allowed time for detecting/isolating/replacing faulty items | hours | MaxAT |
| Fault Coverage | dimensionless | FC |
| Mean Time To Restore (corrective maintenance) | hours | MTTR |
| Mean Time To Maintain (preventive) | hours | MTTM |
| Mean Time To Maintain (corrective) | hours | MTTM |
| Mean Time Between Maintenance (preventive) | time, distance, cycles | MTBM |
| Mean Time Between Maintenance (corrective) | time, distance, cycles | MTBM |

Requirements may be **general** (all maintenance actions) or **specific** (per sub-system).

**Table 11 — Logistic Support Requirements:** MaxAT (hours) — time to reach maintenance site; MaxAT (hours, sub-index pm) — time to plan maintenance actions (spare parts from storage, tools and diagnosis equipment); **SOR** (dimensionless) — probability of spare parts on stock when needed; **TAT** (hours/time) — turn-around time for repairable spares; **lead time** for non-repairable spares. NOTE: if time to reach site / plan actions is not measurable, contractual fixed times may be used.

**Table 12 — Maintenance Cost Requirements:** T (personnel training), T (travel to maintenance sites), SP (spare-parts acquisition/provision/storage), PM (preventive actions incl. spares, software, personnel), CM (corrective actions incl. spares, software, personnel) — all in Money. Alternatives: max allowed values within an agreed period; cost per kilometre / per 1000 km / per year / per vehicle-train / per seat; the Customer specifies exactly which items are counted. Targets may be set for important subsystems (e.g. **bogie, power supply module, doors, air-conditioning, toilets**).

## 7. Availability targets (5.3.4, Table 13; based on Annexes A and C of EN 50126-1)

Availability A = fraction of time the rolling stock is in a state to perform its mission; **A = MUT / (MUT + MDT)**, 0 ≤ A ≤ 1; downtime within interval T: **d(T) = (1 − A) × T**; dimensionless, usually a percentage. Five types by choice of MUT/MDT:

| Type | MUT | MDT | Formula (Table 13) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inherent Availability, Ai** | MTBF (mean time between failures, h) | MTTR (mean time to restore, h) | Ai = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR) |
| **Achieved (technical) Availability, Aa** | MTBM (h) | MTTM (h) — mean time to maintain covering **preventive + corrective** maintenance | Aa = MTBM / (MTBM + MTTM) |
| **Operational (logistic) Availability, Ao** | MTBM (preventive + corrective) | MTTM (h) incl. **logistical and administrative delays** | Ao = MTBM / (MTBM + MTTM) |
| **Fleet Availability, FA** | — | — | FA = F / Ftot = (Ftot − Fm) / Ftot |
| **Schedule Adherence, SA** | — | — | SA = F / Ftot_s = (Ftot_s − F_ns) / Ftot_s |

with F = rolling stock available for service; **Fm** = rolling stock unavailable due to corrective + preventive maintenance; **Ftot** = fleet size; **F** = journeys running to schedule; **F_ns** = journeys not running to schedule **due to rolling-stock-related causes**; **Ftot_s** = total scheduled journeys.

Examples of logistical/administrative delays counted in MTTM: **wait in the depot, wait for spare parts, time to prepare maintenance actions, time necessary to shunt rolling stock**.

**Customer duties when specifying availability:** detail the factors of the chosen formula; specify and account for **responsibility for each factor**; state the monitored time interval; consider system-identification characteristics, economic implications and the practical measurability of RAM requirements from field operations given his organisational/logistical structure and service procedures.

> Not present in this document: the **UR (use-related) vs IR (inherent-related) availability** distinction, **MTBUR (Mean Time Between Unscheduled Removals)** and MTR — these come from other RAM sources (e.g. UNIFE/RailFast practice; EN 50126-1 terminology). This guide uses only the Ai/Aa/Ao/FA/SA types above. MTTR here = Mean Time To Restore (all logistics for the corrective action at train level); distinguish from per-item distance-based measures only via MDBF/MKBF.

## 8. RAM Programme contents & analysis templates (5.5.3, 5.5.4)

**Typical RAM Programme outline (5.5.3):** 1 Introduction (purpose, scope, reference documents, definitions/acronyms); 2 Description of the system (general description, system breakdown); 3 RAM contractual requirements (qualitative: reliability/maintainability/availability; quantitative: same three); 4 RAM management (implementation of RAM; management of interactions between RAM analyses and Quality Plan; configuration-management process; organisation and responsibilities; RAM main activities — system life-cycle phases, RAM activities per phase, RAM documentation); 5 RAM Programme Plan (assumptions and scope of analyses; methods and tools used; RAM detailed activities/analyses/documents: periodical RAM Programme reviews, system condition and mission profile, **reliability modelling, prediction and apportionment**, FMECA analysis and Reliability Block Diagram, Fault Tree Analysis, Software Reliability analysis, corrective maintenance analysis, preventive maintenance analysis, fault isolation and troubleshooting plan, reliability development/growth testing programme, maintainability preliminary tests, reliability demonstration tests, maintainability demonstration tests, **failure data collection from field**; follow-up of RAM critical items); 6 RAM deliverable documents and schedule (list of deliverables, schedule for RAM analyses, periodical RAM activities report).

**Common analysis data (5.5.4.1, Table 14):** analysis header = Rolling stock ID; L1 Code / L1 assembly; L2 Code / L2 assembly; Drawing or diagram reference (identifying LRUs by code and description); Doc. N.; Version Number; Date; Page n/N; Drawn up by; Filename. A **three-level breakdown** is assumed in the examples, level 3 = LRU.

**Reliability prediction sheets (5.5.4.2, Table 15):** Code, Description, Part No., **Failure rate (failures/Mh)**, **MTBF (h)**, Qty (LRUs per L2 assembly), **Total failure rate (failures/Mh)** = LRU failure rate × qty, **Total MTBF (h)**, **Total failure rate (failures/Mkm)**, **Total MKBF (km)**, **Total Qty** (LRUs in the whole rolling stock), **Source of failure rate** (incl. data-source codes), Notes (origin, correction factors). Where failure rate is in failures/Mkm, **MDBF/MKBF is used**. Example sheet: Figure 1.

**Maintenance attributes (5.5.4.3) — maintenance levels, vehicles decisions, skill levels, LRU:**
- **In-Service Maintenance** (depot personnel/resources/spares/tools minimise standstill) vs **Out-of-Service Maintenance** (specialised depot/workshop; rolling stock out of service for the duration).
- Preventive-maintenance maintenance-level codes: **InS-PM** (all scheduled actions in the depot, or only remove/install in the depot with other actions off-train), **OutS-PM**.
- Corrective-maintenance maintenance-level codes: **InS-CM-Rep** (repairable failure mode, all repair actions in depot), **InS-CM-NotRep** (non-repairable failure mode: only remove/install in depot), **OutS-CM** (repairable failure mode; both remove/install and repair in depot/workshop).
- **LRU definition:** "the item on which In-Service maintenance actions are carried out" — used once the breakdown-structure depth is defined.
- **Skill level codes:** **"L" Low** (basic knowledge; directly visible/easily accessible items; standard tools: screwdriver, wrench); **"I" Intermediate** (specific knowledge; searching for the item; non-standard tools: multimeter, gauge, tester; disassembling with diagrams and maintenance manuals); **"H" High** (complete specialised knowledge; sophisticated tools: oscilloscope, logic state analyser; fine tuning and disassembling with drawings, diagrams, maintenance manuals).

**Preventive maintenance analysis sheets (5.5.4.4, Table 16; Figures 2 and 3):** Code, Description, Part No., Qty, Step no., Maintenance task, Spare parts & special tools (equipment not provided by depot/workshop and not easily market-available, consumables and spares), **Frequency** (time or km), Maintenance level, **Personnel** (number simultaneously required), **Skill level (L/I/H)**, Spare-parts cost (EURO), **Man-hours** (task duration × number of persons), **Standstill time**, Total Qty, Notes. Header adds assembly totals: spare-parts cost, man-hour cost and total cost **per 1000 km (€/1000 km)**. Customer defines man-hour cost. **Preventive Maintenance Sheet for a single frequency (Fig. 3):** rearranged data for all LRUs maintained in one frequency interval, plus frequency-total logistics data: spare-parts cost, man-hours, standstill time and **maximum number of persons simultaneously involved** — a direct aid for building the maintenance plan/logistic organisation.

**Corrective maintenance analysis sheets (5.5.4.5, Table 17; Figure 4):** as Table 16 plus **Failure Mode** (chained to the FMECA sheet) and **Failure rate (failures/Mh)** of the LRU failure mode; same personnel/skill/cost/standstill data; assembly-total cost per 1000 km in header.

**FMECA sheets (5.5.4.6, Table 18; Figures 5 and 6):** items FMECA = Code, Description, Part No., Qty, Function, **Failure Mode** (predicted or observed result of a failure cause under the operating conditions at failure), **Failure Cause** (circumstances that led to the failure), Failure rate (f/Mh), effects: **Local** / **on higher assembly (L1/L2)** / **on rolling stock** (incl. effects on other assemblies), **Criticality** (code per stated reference table, based on effects affecting **safety and/or service**), **Failure Category** (Fail codes), **Diagnosis** (detection/identification method incl. diagnostics), **Preventive/Corrective Actions**, Notes. Preventive/corrective actions must be addressed by: the **designer** (when FMECA is done in the design phase, to improve design) and by the **"on board" and maintenance personnel** (in Maintenance and User Manuals, to improve service). **Functions FMECA** (Fig. 6): same sheet re-cast for functions — drop structure columns; add function reference (ID of the functional block diagram), **N°** (function number within the diagram), Function, Sub-function, Phase.

## 9. RAM assurance per phase (Cl. 6)

### 6.1.1 Tender Phase (EN 50126-1 phases 1–5)
Customer prepares the **call for tender** containing rolling-stock concept/definition/specification and the RAM requirements per Clause 5, and should request **RAM data and analyses** from potential Main Suppliers to compare offers on a common basis (documentation may be requested like design-phase documents, cf. 5.5.4). Main Supplier tasks **before answering**:
- **Feasibility analysis:** determine the best reasonable achievable performances from current experience and past data, accounting for differences in the new mission profile and/or new system definition; at rolling-stock level using similar-application experience and analysis models such as **Fault Tree Analysis** or **Reliability Block Diagrams**;
- **Commercial risk analysis:** using the best reasonable achievable performances, calculate the **probability of incurring penalties** given the gap between required and achievable performances;
- **Response to the tender:** produce requested RAM documents and the RAM sections of the offer;
- **Negotiation:** answer queries/questions and support the negotiation team.
The phase ends with evaluation, a (usually short) negotiation confirming Contractual Agreements, and **Contract Award or Notice To Proceed**.

### 6.1.2 Design Phase (phase 6; Fig. 9 and Table 19)
Flow: RAM specification → Preliminary analyses → **Preliminary Design Review** → Detailed analyses → **Final Design Review** → RAM Growth monitoring + **Follow-up of critical items** → final synthesis report → Acceptance & Commissioning (feeding into Demonstration Phase). Inputs: tender documentation + contractual agreements; data from test and field loops back in; an **Action Plan** resolves problems identified.

**Table 19 — main tasks:**
- **RAM specification:** after Contract Award, the Main Supplier specifies to Sub-Contractors/Partners/Equipment suppliers the contractual requirements for subsystems, functions or equipment in their scope of supply, generally including RAM Programme delivery requirements for approval.
- **Preliminary analyses:** inputs = tender documents + contractual agreements; implemented at **functional level** to highlight critical RAM functions for subsequent detailed analyses; functions are classified using the main supplier's failure categories adapted to the contract; performed with **functional FMECAs** and **Reliability Block Diagrams**; deliverable = set of Preliminary Analyses covering all RAM Requirements; depth limited to breakdown levels with sufficiently detailed design data.
- **Preliminary Design Review:** milestone to check design progress to preliminary stage; review RAM documentation to keep RAM activities in step with design progress, confirm requirements are being achieved, examine arising problems.
- **Detailed analyses:** perform the analyses highlighted by preliminary analyses; by now the design has progressed to the **LRU level** of the breakdown structure; update previous analyses; issues: resolve problems from preceding analysis/review, verify document completeness, prepare test and field-data collection activities. The Main Supplier collects sub-contractor detailed analyses to: ensure good consistency; **integrate their equipment-level predictions into the model to get rolling-stock-level predictions; integrate interface mismatches** into the model.
- **Final Design Review:** check design completion; confirm all RAM requirements properly achieved; resolve outstanding problems; verify document completeness; prepare tests and field-data collection.
- **RAM Growth monitoring:** monitor RAM (essentially reliability) growth so that the time to reach the **steady-state level in revenue service is as short as possible**; a cross-party problem-solving process: identify problems occurring in the earliest operation phase, highlight top problem contributors affecting contractual service performance, implement an action plan to eradicate/mitigate, follow up the impact and decide whether to continue the iterative process.
- **Follow-up of critical items:** continuous management task from preliminary analysis to service operation: identify critical scenarios (failures affecting service or safety); rank them by estimated frequency of occurrence combined with effects on safety or service level; propose/ask designers for actions or provisions to eliminate scenarios or mitigate effects; estimate the impact of the action plan; follow up application during later design phases and manufacturing. Performed with **problem-solving techniques**.
- Reliability growth/maintainability demonstration test slots and failure-data collection feed from Test and Field data (Fig. 9). Provisional calculation of RAM parameters in this phase is "usually based on the use of **probabilistic figures**".

### 6.1.3 Demonstration Phase (EN 50126-1 phases 9–10–11; 6.1.3.1–6.1.3.4)
- **Introduction:** the operation period in which the Customer checks fulfilment of RAM contractual targets through **RAM Demonstration tests and collection of field data**. Field-data collection provides the Customer: a basis for **rolling-stock acceptance**; knowledge of performance; a **reference database for new tenders**. It enables the Main Supplier to: monitor/verify design, manufacture and installation; monitor operation to address corrective actions; gather data/knowledge for further development. For long-term operation: find weak points of the management process (operation and maintenance) as a basis for performance improvement and cost reduction, and optimise maintenance/operation. Test-procedure definitions must be **consistent with the system identification/definition of Clause 5**. All RAM documents from the design phase are intended as **proof of compliance** with RAM Requirements.
- **RAM Acceptance Criteria (6.1.3.2):** Customer defines RAM acceptance criteria for the field based on the RAM requirements chosen; reliability acceptance criteria must specify conditions under which a failure is **"chargeable"** (Main Supplier responsibility) or **"non-chargeable"**. Failures to be considered **non-chargeable** (when verified): failures occurring while **operating outside the stated service conditions**; failures due to **Customer personnel**; failures due to **accidental events**. For each RAM requirement, detailed acceptance criteria must be defined **before the beginning of the checking period** — clear rules to be followed.
- **In-service checking period (6.1.3.3):** duration defined by the Customer, considering that RAM targets are "usually not achieved in the first period of operation but after a **burn-in period**, during which a **reliability growth monitoring process** should be implemented — **the steady-state level is generally achieved at least after two years of revenue service**". Reliability growth monitoring = continuous monitoring of field data, successive RAM analysis and matching against contractual targets; additional requirements may be agreed. Key rules: corrective actions within the growth process must be managed as in 5.5 (with a corrective-action plan, as appropriate); **the growth process should be concluded before the RAM checking period starts**; the rolling-stock **configuration status should be stable and frozen** during checking (any needed technical modification — e.g. **software update** — must have its consequences precisely quantified by the Supplier). Duration choice should account for: fleet size and **cumulative annual kilometres of service**; the maintenance plan; the RAM requirements chosen; the **duration of the contractual relationship (i.e. duration of the warranty period)**. The Customer considers whether checking/data collection covers **the entire fleet, a selected number of vehicles, or other systems/subsystems of interest**.
- **Field data report & organisation (6.1.3.4):** gathered data comprises: failures during **operation and maintenance**; **failure classification (Significant, Major, Minor)**; **structure code** of the failing item; fault description; work during maintenance; **man-hours**; material consumption; **kilometres/hours of service**; downtimes and standstill times (incl. logistic and administrative delays). Reports on repair and cause of failure are needed to drive corrective action. Identification/traceability data: report ID, event date, operation-status description, **vehicle ID within the fleet**; optionally preliminary probable-cause info with technical evaluation for acceptance-criteria allocation. Customer and Main Supplier usually agree a **RAM Demonstration Plan** specifying at least: rules and methods; resources and tools; organisation and responsibility; **field-data collection sites (depots, workshops)**; reference documents (RAM analyses and breakdown structure); RAM contractual targets; acceptance criteria; data-collection procedure; detection of all data sources and disturbances; **schedule of periodical field-data-collection reviews**; procedure to resolve problems and address corrective actions; procedure for implementing the corrective-action plan. The corrective-action plan must also assess impacts on the **configuration-management process** (5.5.2).

## 10. Specific hazards related to rolling stock (Cl. 7)

- **Purpose (7.1):** a reference list of the **most common hazards** associated with rolling stock, needed for **risk analysis as defined in 4.6.2 and 6.3 of EN 50126-1**. Safety treatment here is intentionally minimal — the list is an aid only; a suitable and coherent hazard list for the rolling stock under consideration must be developed from it.
- **Hazard identification process (7.2):** hazards must be identified with the **full range of competencies** involved, covering at least: type of operation; procedures for **signalling**; modes of operation; prevailing rules, regulations and legal constraints; applicable standards; technical and functional specification; maintenance and logistic support issues; **human factors**; environment and functional-interface issues. Aim: identify situations, failures, errors or events adversely affecting safety — **injury or death of passengers and/or service personnel** when they are: on the rolling stock; boarding/alighting; on or near the line; on the station; performing maintenance. Use of data from previous **accidents and incidents**, generic lists and safety/hazard documents is recommended.
- **Hazard list (7.3)** grouped by category (intentionally repetitive across categories):
  - **Electrical:** tensioned surfaces/points; overheating; ignition of combustibles; induced voltage (capacitive coupling); electrostatic discharge; inadvertent activation; electrical arc.
  - **Mechanical:** sharp edges/points; lifting weights; vibration; rotating equipment; stability/toppling potential; altered structural properties; reciprocating equipment; ejected/thrown parts & fragments; pinch points; crushing surfaces.
  - **Pneumatic/Hydraulic Pressure:** over-pressurisation; blown objects; pipe/vessel/duct rupture; cross-flow; pipe/hose whip; implosion; backflow/siphon effect; blast.
  - **Acceleration/Deceleration/Gravity:** excessive acceleration; excessive deceleration; inadvertent motion; loose-object translation; **derailment; collision**; slip/trip; falling objects; falls.
  - **Temperature Extremes:** hot/cold surface; freezing; confined gas/liquid; reduced reliability; pressure elevation; humidity/moisture; elevated flammability; elevated volatility.
  - **Fire/Flammability/Noise:** presence of noise, fuel, ignition source, oxidiser, radiation, explosive propellant/gas/liquid/vapour/dust, compressed air/gas, lubrication.
  - **Leaks/Spills / Materials:** liquids/cryogens; gases/vapours; dusts; **toxic, irritants, corrosive, asphyxiates, allergens**.
  - **Human Factors (see also Ergonomic):** operator error; operation out of sequence; inadvertent operation; right operation/wrong control; failure to operate; operate too long; operation early/late; operate too briefly.
  - **Ergonomic:** fatigue; inaccessibility; inadequate control/readout differentiation; inappropriate control/readout location; faulty/inadequate control/readout labelling; inadequate/improper illumination; glare; non-existent/inadequate "kill" switches; faulty workstation design.
  - **Control Systems:** power outage; **sneak software**; interference (EMI/ESI); lightning strike; moisture; grounding failure; **sneak circuit**; inadvertent activation.
- **Not present:** no TFFR/THR/SIL machinery, no safety-acceptance classes, no references to EN 50155 (railway electronics), EN 61373 (shock/vibration), or fire-protection standards in this TR — those interfaces live in EN 50126-1, in CLC/TR 50126-2 (safety guide, in preparation at publication date) and the sector product standards.

## 11. LCC (Cl. 8)

- **8.1:** LCC = "process of economic analysis to assess the total cost of acquisition and ownership of a product"; inputs to design/development/use decisions; Main Suppliers use LCC to **optimise designs, perform trade-off studies and evaluate operating/maintenance strategies**; also a value to compare offers and control the project. "**As RAM requirements influence cost of rolling stock, they are the basis of any Life Cycle Cost calculation.**"
- **8.2 — LCC modelling basics:** a simplified representation of the rolling stock's main features as cost figures. Basic elements: (1) the **physical structure** (→ breakdown structure & boundary limits, 5.1.4, and **system identification**, 5.1.3) and **mission profile**; (2) the relevant **lifetime phases** (per EN 50126-1); (3) the relevant **cost categories**. Total costs group under: **Acquisition costs; Ownership costs; Disposal costs**; detailed subcategories derive from this outline. Full model development is out of scope — for detailed development see **IEC 60300-3-3**; also the **UNIFE LCC GROUP series Volumes I–IV** (bibliography).
- **8.3 — RAM parameters for LCC:** Tables 5/6 "conditions" columns (delay thresholds, cost thresholds) are direct LCC levers; failure rate / MTBF / MTTF / MDBF (Table 8) impact LCC via **maintenance cost** — one of the most significant LCC items. **Corrective maintenance** cost grows with age (after the early-failure period); **preventive maintenance** cost peaks in years with expensive actions (major inspections, expensive-part replacement, refurbishing, overhaul — carried out only **two to five times** over service life), so preventive costs must be evaluated over the same basis to **compare different operators/suppliers equally**. Cost parameters (from Figures 1–3 / Tables 16–17 elements): spare-parts costs (new or reconditioned); working time per maintenance activity (man-hours); man-hour cost; maintenance interval for each preventive action within the lifetime plan; quantities in the breakdown structure; reliability of the maintained item (failure rate, MTBF/MTTF/MDBF). **Availability-related costs** are the hardest: consequences of unavailability = lost revenue; cost of alternative service; loss of image/reputation/prestige (→ loss of clients) — typically commercial/marketing matters with no direct link to 5.3.4; but when the estimate is built on **down-state time (hours/days)** or **journeys not operated**, the parameters of **Table 13** apply directly.

## 14. Quick-use checklist

- To specify RAM for rolling stock: run the 5.1 preliminary analysis (system ID per 5.1.3 + breakdown structure per 5.1.4) → define failure conditions/categories with thresholds and delay-counting method (5.2, Tables 4–7) → set reliability targets per Table 8 (FR/MTBF/MTTF/MDBF with I/S/M suffixes), maintainability targets per Tables 9–12 (incl. MTTR/MTBM/MTTM/FC, MinInt, MaxST, SOR, TAT), availability targets per Table 13 (Ai/Aa/Ao/FA/SA with factor responsibilities and monitoring interval) → document the figure-choice process (5.4).
- To demonstrate: RAM Programme (5.5.3 outline) → tender-phase feasibility + commercial risk (penalty probability, 6.1.1) → design-phase preliminary/functional analyses, LRU-level detail, sub-supplier integration incl. interface mismatches (6.1.2) → RAM growth monitoring to steady state (typically ≥ 2 years of revenue service) → frozen configuration → chargeable/non-chargeable acceptance criteria agreed before the checking period (6.1.3.2) → in-service checking period whose duration is set against fleet km, maintenance plan, requirements and **warranty duration** (6.1.3.3) → field-data collection per 6.1.3.4 under a RAM Demonstration Plan.
- Failure taxonomy engine: Significant (FailI) = delay > threshold / stopped on track / withdrawn / not allowed into service / cost > threshold; Major (FailS) = must be rectified & delay/cost under the significant thresholds; Minor (FailM) = maintenance-task-generating, no service impact. Count only **chargeable** failures against targets; non-chargeable = outside stated service conditions / Customer personnel / accidental events.

# EN 50126-1:2017 — Generic RAMS Process — Full Reference

BS EN 50126-1:2017 (CENELEC Oct 2017), "Railway applications — The specification and demonstration of RAMS — Part 1: Generic RAMS process". Supersedes EN 50126-1:1999. No normative references (self-contained; vocabulary from IEC 60050-192/-821/-903/-351; EN 50128 referenced for definitions of COTS/pre-existing software). Railway-sector application of IEC 61508;; compliance with EN 50126 + other suitable standards is sufficient — no separate IEC 61508 demonstration.

## 1. Scope (Cl. 1)
Process for **managing RAMS** built on the system life cycle; specification & demonstration of RAMS requirements; tailorable. Fields: **Command, Control & Signalling; Rolling Stock; Fixed Installations**; all levels incl. software. Applies to new systems, new systems integrated into accepted systems (new function extent only), modifications/extensions (modified extent only). Does NOT define application-specific RAMS targets/quantities, certification rules, or approval processes. Not required for unmodified existing systems. Annex ZZ presumption of conformity with Directive 2008/57/EC TSIs (CCS 2016/919, LOC&PAS 1302/2014, Energy 1301/2014, Infrastructure 1299/2014) — applied together with EN 50126-2.

## 2. RAMS qualities & inter-relationship (Cl. 3, 5.4/5.5)
- Reliability 3.52: ability to perform as required without failure, given time/conditions (time may be cycles/distance). Availability 3.6: ability to be in a state to perform at a given instant/interval assuming external resources. Maintainability 3.37: retained/restored under given use & maintenance conditions. Safety 3.64: freedom from unacceptable risk.
- Interdependence (5.4/5.5): managed jointly — conflict resolution policy is part of the Phase-2 RAMS policy; availability rests on reliability + maintainability + O&M; safety rests additionally on all hazards/accidents, safety-related failure modes, maintainability & O&M of safety-related parts.
- **RAM risk vs safety risk (5.4, 6.3)**: RAM risk = loss of service value (delays/cancellations; "RAM equivalent of a hazard" 6.3 NOTE 1); safety risk = harm. Security separate; quality-of-service downstream of RAMS; LCC strongly RAM-driven.
- Failure taxonomy (5.6.2): **random** (statistically estimable → design/derating/quality/condition monitoring) vs **systematic** (human error in life-cycle activities → controlled by processes/methods/organisation; Clauses 6–7 largely aim at preventing systematic failures). Human factors 5.6.4 (random vs systematic human error, competence, HSI, culture).

## 3. Life cycle (6.2, 6.5, Cl. 7) — **12 phases in the 2017 edition**
Three blocks (6.2, Fig. 6): ① **Risk assessment** (incl. specification of RAMS requirements) — basis = system definition; ② **Implementation and demonstration of compliance**; ③ **Operation, maintenance and decommissioning**. Feedback loop: updated risk knowledge triggers reassessment (from Phase 3; worst case reopen Phase 1 remit); risk assessment re-confirmed at end of life cycle. V-model in Fig. 7; sub-level systems repeat the phases (6.5.2) with interface assumptions stated Phase 2, constraints Phase 5, exported/imported **SRACs managed in Integration**.

## 4. RAM process
- Targets Phase 4 from system definition + risk analysis; agreed within legal framework (5.7.2). **Annex B parameters**: λ, MTBF/MTTF, MUT; MDT/MTBM/MTTR, FC, RC; A, Ai, Ao, Fleet Availability, Schedule Adherence; logistic support (O&MC, MLD, MAD, MMH, SPS); safety: hazard rate, **wrong-side failure probability (pWSF)**, time to safe state. Availability with delay structure per Annex B Fig. B.1 (IEC 61703-based math).
- Apportionment Phase 5: cascaded down hierarchy with iterations to optimise the whole.
- **RAM plan (7.3.2.2)**: management (life-cycle RAM tasks & processes; **FRACAS from Phase 7**; RAM deliverables; RAM acceptance tasks; constraints; subcontractor management) + reliability (analysis/prediction/planning/testing/data acquisition) + availability + maintainability (+ logistic support evaluation). Informative Table 1 gives tasks per phase. Annex A: 5-step plan-building procedure + example RAMS plan (Table A.1; LCC estimation Phase-6 task; LCC assessment Phase-11 task).
- RAM risk strategies (5.9.3): reliability (tolerances, derating, quality, condition monitoring/PM) and availability (redundancy/back-up Ph.5; degraded modes Ph.2/5; maintainability Ph.6; resources Ph.11).

## 5. Safety process
- **Hazard identification classes (7.4.2.1)**: normal operation, fault conditions, emergency operation, foreseeable misuse, interfaces, functionality, **configuration parameters**, O&M/support, disposal, human factors, occupational health, mechanical/electrical/natural environments. Structured checklists; consequence chain definitions.
- **Risk evaluation & RAP (6.3)**: broadly-acceptable screen (recorded; bounded share of overall risk; expert judgment), else RAP = ① Code of Practice / ② similar reference system / ③ Explicit Risk Estimation with RAC (set by legal framework or duty holder). Safety requirements then specified. THR defined per hazard in per-hour terms, per **instance** of function (C.4 example; multiple-instance caveat for e.g. door command); example: credible catastrophic function failures acceptable without further reduction if rate ≤ **1×10⁻⁹ per operating hour**. Frequency & severity tables live in Annex C.
- **Annex C (numbers)**: 6 frequency classes (Frequent, Probable ≈ 6w–1y, .., Highly improbable) incl. distance-based F1–F3 variants; RAM severity (Significant-immobilising / Major-service / Minor); RAMS severity (Catastrophic/Critical/Marginal/Insignificant); **safety severity S1 (many equivalent fatalities) → S5 (minor injury)**; financial SF1–SF3; **equivalent-fatality convention: 1 fatality ≈ 10 major ≈ 100 minor** (agreed with duty holder); risk acceptance matrices: 2-tier Unacceptable/Acceptable or 4-tier **Intolerable/Undesirable/Tolerable/Negligible**.
- **Safety risk reduction order (5.9.2)**: inherent safety (safe failure modes; note safe states relative — stop-in-tunnel example) → safety functions/barriers (checked at sufficient intervals) → safety-related information/constraints. Prevention preferred (5.9.1).
- **Safety plan (7.3.2.3)**: policy/strategy, scope, planning, life-cycle safety tasks, relations to assessment, **role independence**, hazard identification & analysis, risk assessment & ongoing management, RAC definition & review, effectiveness review of measures, safety-requirement adequacy review, design, verification, validation, audits, safety assurance of parameterisation, deliverables list, **safety case preparation process**, safety approval process (duty holder & safety authority interface), safety monitoring of O&M performance, documentation maintenance, **hazard log management process**, interfaces, constraints/assumptions, subcontractors, periodic audits/reviews with independence.
- **SIL (3.70)**: discrete safety-integrity levels of safety-related functions (higher = more integrity; not allocatable to processes/measures); **basic integrity (3.7)** = TFFR > 10⁻⁵/h or non-safety-related. Interpretation/assignment machinery = EN 50126-2. Validation depth & ISA rigor scale with integrity (7.10.2, 6.8).

## 6. V&V, quality, independence (6.4–6.8)
- Verification (6.7.2) each phase; validation (6.7.3) at Phases 4 & 9; validation entity explicitly defined and independent (EN 50126-2 Cl. 7); plans justify the validation strategy; deviation/non-conformance traced.
- QMS conform to EN ISO 9001-equivalent; enough for RAM quality; necessary basis for safety management; RAMS process embedded in organisation's management system; configuration management normative.
- Roles independence (6.4): designer/verifier/validator/assessor; bigger risk ⇒ more independence; role freedom from commercial pressure.
- Tailoring (6.5): extent in the RAMS plan; always normative: responsibilities, competence, **RAM plan + Safety plan**, QMS, configuration mgmt; explicit justified omissions; special cases: multi-level application, renewals, re-use/cross-acceptance (7-step argument 6.5.4), COTS/pre-existing SW.

## 7. Safety case (Cl. 8; EN 50126-2 types)
Minimum content 8.2: ① system definition (architecture, interfaces, environment, safety requirements, configuration/version, input requirements incl. risk analyses); ② **Quality Management Report**; ③ **Safety Management Report**; ④ **Technical Safety Report** (fault-free operation; failures/errors; external influences; **SRAC**); ⑤ related safety cases with SRAC fulfillment/carry-forward; ⑥ Conclusion — adequately safe subject to SRAC compliance. Safety case types (GP/GA/SA) and dependencies = EN 50126-2 Cl. 6.

## 8. Deliverable cross-table
RAM plan (Ph.2, living) · Safety plan (Ph.2, living) · Hazard Log (Ph.3 onward; operational log Ph.11) · RAMS requirements spec, acceptance criteria, SRAC (Ph.4/5/…) · Validation plans (Ph.4/5) & reports (Ph.4, Ph.9) · RAM analysis/hazard analysis/safety case (Ph.6; maintained) · QA reports (Ph.7) · integration report (Ph.8) · validation reports (Ph.9) · acceptance report & ISA report (Ph.10) · FRACAS records, performance analyses, operational hazard log, change impact analyses (Ph.11) · decommissioning report (Ph.12). Document management 6.6: unique numbered versions, traceability, configuration control, acronym consistency.

## Notes for 1999-edition users
- 14 phases (1999) → 12 (2017): "Installation" + confirmation steps folded into Integration/System Validation.
- 1999 "Safety Criticality Categories" replaced by broad-acceptable screening + S1–S5/severity + RAC/THR; SIL machinery lives in EN 50126-2.
- FRACAS now explicit from Phase 7 (RAM plan) and normative in Phase 11; **SRAC** is a central recurring artifact; safety case is a structured six-part document.

# IEC 60300-3-3:2017 — Life Cycle Costing — Full Reference

IEC 60300-3-3, Edition 3.0, 2017-01. Committee IEC TC 56. Bilingual EN/FR. No normative references (self-contained); part of the dependability management framework of IEC 60300-1. Ed 3 vs 2004: complete analysis process added; international accounting practice (GAAP/IFRS) referenced; deeper financial concepts.

## 1. Scope & purpose
- General introduction to LCC for all applications, **highlighting costs associated with dependability**; audience includes managers, engineers, finance staff, contractors.
- **LCC = total cost incurred during the life cycle** (whole-life cost); **life cycle costing** = the economic analysis process over the life cycle or a portion.
- Principal use: **comparing alternative system solutions** where future ownership cost is significant — balancing acquisition cost vs the *residual unrealized risk of ownership* via assessments of **availability, reliability, maintainability, supportability**.
- LCC covers **only expense elements** (tangible + intangible); revenues/value are excluded (financial trade-off follows subsequently). Costs include **allowance for residual risk**.

## 2. Life cycle & cost categories
- Six generic stages (Annex A.1): concept → development → realization → **utilization** → enhancement → retirement. First three = acquisition cost; last three = ownership cost (O&S-type). Ownership cost includes **unrealized risk costs**.
- Fig A.2 committed-cost curve: ~35/75/95/99/100 % committed by end-of-stage; uncertainty band ×0.5→×2.5.
- Acquisition = **NRC** (non-recurring: facilities, tooling/test equipment, initial spares, initial training, documentation, software, qualification testing) vs **RC** (recurring: production management, fabrication, QC/inspection, assembly, installation, shipping, ongoing training, insurance) — allocation depends on perspective (manufacturer vs user).
- Definitions (Cl. 3): CBS (cost breakdown structure); cost element; cost driver (structur highest-attention); base date; discount rate; intangible item (per IAS 38); liability cost; useful life; time value of money. Acronyms: CBS, CU, DCF, GAAP, IFRS, IRR, kCU, LCC, LORA, MTBF/MOTBF, MTTF, NPV, VAT.

## 3. Financial machinery (Annex C)
- **Compounding (C.6)**: SV = PS(1+R)ⁿ — worked: CU 100 @ 5 % → CU 127.63.
- **DCF/NPV (C.2)**: discount future cost-flow streams; caution on inflation-risk sensitivity; PV = F/(1+r)ⁿ is implicit, not verbatim-restated in the text.
- **IRR (C.3)**: discount rate at which NPV = 0; invest if IRR ≥ required return.
- **Discount rate (5.3.4)**: WACC-based company rate **including an inflation component**; finance function determines exact values; base date agreed up front.
- **Tax (B.5.3)**: deduct indirect VAT-type taxes from market prices; include direct taxes/tariffs as costs; no double taxation. **Opportunity cost (B.5.2)** of early dependability investment must be accounted. Exchange-rate risk per contract terms (B.5.4). GAAP usual, IFRS alternative (B.5.5). **Depreciation usually ignored for LCC purposes** (C.4/C.5 note).

## 4. Process (Cl. 5, Fig. 1; Cl. 6)
Iterative five steps with alternatives/objectives loops:
1. **Establish organizational context (5.2)**: contextual statement; future scenarios; identify alternatives.
2. **Plan the analysis (5.3)**: scope/objectives (absolute vs comparative); evaluation criteria NPV/payback/IRR; **CBS as matrix of cost elements × stages**; tasks/personnel; constraints; financial parameters.
3. **Define approach (5.4)**: 5.4.1 rules (exclude costs common to options); 5.4.2 **one model for all alternatives, math verified**; 5.4.3 **CBS 3-D matrix = WBS × life-cycle stages × cost categories** [labour, materials, fuel/energy, overheads, transport]; cost drivers; LCC database; 5.4.4 uncertainty treatment.
4. **Perform (5.5)**: 5.5.1 estimate methods (parametric, analogous, engineering — Annex F mirrors); 5.5.2 cost data collection/allocation/validation; 5.5.3 aggregation = simple sum over CBS; 5.5.4 LCC + sensitivity; 5.5.5 independent review; 5.5.6 objectives check.
5. **Finalize (Cl. 6)**: follow-up actions with named owners (6.1); documentation: model-objective alignment, accuracy, data quality, traceability, validation (6.2).

## 5. Dependability ↔ LCC (4.4, B.2)
- Reliability ↑ ⇒ acquisition ↑ but O&M ↓ ⇒ **optimum reliability at minimum LCC exists**; maintainability ↓ cost + ↓ downtime; supportability/logistics improve LCC. **LORA** = canonical acquisition-vs-utilization trade-off.
- Availability optimization is critical because unavailability causes **consequential costs**: lost revenue, alternative service costs, warranty, liability, reputation.
- Trade-off factors (C.5): operational & intrinsic availability; spares cost; manpower cost; mission-success probability.
- **Annex G worked example (Weibull life, β = 2.0, η = 20 000 h pumps)** — pump-domain LCC comparison: redundancy reduces downtime (32 h / <0.1 h / 3.2 h / 1 h across options) and total LCC 893.4 / 479.5 / 488 / **337.8 kCU** — the cheapest-to-buy option has the highest LCC; the cheapest-overall option (4) has highest hardware cost.

## 6. Estimation worked examples (Annex F)
- **Parametric (F.2)**: Cost = Q × Z(failures/yr) × CL(labour rate/h) × n(persons) × MRT = 1 × 0.3 × 50 × 1 × 2.4 = CU 36/yr; inflation/discount applied per element.
- **Analogous (F.3)**: 220 × 1.05 + 50 = CU 281.
- **Engineering (F.4)**: 38.80 h × CU 54.50 = CU 2 114.60.

## 7. Uncertainty & sensitivity (5.4.4, 5.5.4)
- Two uncertainty elements: (a) scoping/model assumptions; (b) data accuracy. Four inaccuracy sources: stochastic MTTF/MTBF-type data; planned O&M risk balancing; recovery costs at indeterminate future time; **untreated unrealized risk cost**.
- Minimum treatment: best-estimate baseline + sensitivity on cost drivers, context and time-value assumptions (min/max ranges); stronger: PDFs or **Monte Carlo** over cost-driver distributions.
